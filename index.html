    How it works

    Six steps from your layout file to a chip on your desk.

    You design in the tools you already use, open-source or commercial. We take it from the finished layout.

    1. STEP 1

      Upload GDS

      Upload your GDS layout file to our portal. GDS is the standard drawing of a chip that every foundry accepts.

    2. STEP 2

      AI pre-check

      Our AI scans the layout for manufacturing-rule errors and explains any problem in plain English, before money is spent.

    3. STEP 3

      AI stitching onto shared chip

      The stitching engine packs designs of different sizes onto one chip with minimal wasted area and assigns the shared pins.

    4. STEP 4

      Fabricate

      The combined chip goes to a foundry as one order, so the manufacturing cost is split across every design.

    5. STEP 5

      Package & test in Penang

      The bare chips are packaged and tested in Penang. You get a test report with your parts.

    6. STEP 6

      Delivered

      Your own packaged chips arrive at your lab or office, ready for the bench.

    What is inside a chip

    Hover or tap the chip to pull it apart

    • Package. The black shell with metal pins that you solder to a circuit board.
    • Die. The sliver of silicon cut from the wafer. This is the part we share.
    • Metal layers. Stacked copper wiring that connects everything together.
    • Transistors. Millions of tiny switches at the bottom that do the actual logic.

    FAQ

    Questions we expect.

    Can my design stay private?

    Yes. Commercial designs are covered by an NDA. Your layout is not published, and the public shuttle map shows your area only as a private design. Student tiles are open source, which keeps them at the lowest price.

    Which processes do you use?

    We start on 130 nm with an open process design kit (PDK). Open means the manufacturing rules are public, so anyone can design for it without foundry paperwork first. More processes follow as the roadmap moves to Malaysian fabs.

    What file do I submit?

    A GDS layout file, the standard format every chip design tool can export. It does not matter whether you made it with open-source or commercial tools. You also tell us which pins your design needs, and the stitching engine assigns them on the shared chip.

    How long until I get chips?

    Plan for roughly six to nine months after a shuttle closes. Fabrication takes most of that time. Packaging and test in Penang come last, and the shuttle tracker shows progress throughout.

    Can universities claim this from training budgets?

    We issue itemised quotations and invoices that separate tiles, workshops and support, so your institution can submit them against its own training or grant budget. Whether a claim is accepted depends on your funder's rules, so check with your finance office before you commit.
